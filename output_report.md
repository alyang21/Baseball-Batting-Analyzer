## Final Summary

## Summary of Findings

I explored bat-speed, swing-quality, and outcome metrics across all 226 hitters. Here's what stood out:

**1. Bat speed is a real weapon, but it's a double-edged sword.**
- Bat speed correlates strongly with whiff rate (**r = 0.63**) and negatively with squared-up contact rate (**r = -0.62**) — swinging harder reliably means missing more and squaring the ball up less often.
- Despite that tradeoff, bat speed still has a **positive overall relationship with run value (r = 0.365)** — the extra damage on contact outweighs the added whiffs, on average, across the league.
- Swing length behaves similarly: longer swings correlate with more bat speed (r = 0.58) and more whiffs (r = 0.41), but only a mild positive tie to run value (r = 0.23).

**2. But bat speed alone doesn't guarantee results — quality/contact interaction matters more than raw exit-swing metrics suggest.**
- Whiff rate (r = 0.085) and squared-up rate (r = -0.024) individually have almost **no correlation with run value**. Blast rate (r = 0.47) and hard-swing rate (r = 0.39) are much better predictors — it's not just about avoiding whiffs, it's about combining bat speed with squared-up, "blasted" contact.
- **Aaron Judge vs. Oneil Cruz** is the clearest illustration: both swing extremely hard (77.0 vs 78.8 mph), have nearly identical whiff rates (32.5% vs 32.4%) and nearly identical contact/squared-up numbers — yet Judge finished with the **best run value in the sample (+34.1)** while Cruz was near the bottom (**-21.1**). Their bat-tracking profiles look almost the same, but results are worlds apart — a reminder that these Statcast swing metrics don't capture everything (sequencing, pitch selection, quality of contact by exit velo/launch angle, etc.).

**3. Elite contact skill is not a shortcut to elite outcomes either.**
- Luis Arraez is a clean opposite case: **lowest bat speed in MLB (62.6 mph)**, lowest hard-swing rate (0.4%), and the **best squared-up rate in the league (42.6%)** with a minuscule whiff rate (5.1%) — yet his run value was still **negative (-22.6)**, similarly poor to Cruz's. Slapping the ball squarely without any power translates to a lot of harmless contact.
- This produces a U-shaped pattern: the best outcomes (Judge, Ohtani, Kurtz, Soto) come from hitters who blend above-average bat speed **with** above-average squared-up/blast rates — not from maximizing either dimension alone. Extreme contact-only or speed-only profiles both land near the bottom of the run-value leaderboard.

**4. Bat speed and pure contact ability are basically independent skills.**
- Correlation between avg_bat_speed and raw contact count is essentially zero (r = -0.024), confirming that swinging hard doesn't inherently cost or gain a hitter contact events league-wide — it depends on the hitter.
- Hard-swing rate and squared-up rate are moderately negatively correlated (r = -0.55), reinforcing the classic power/precision tradeoff at the individual swing level.
